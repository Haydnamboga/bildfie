The reality we refuse to accept

In the Gusii highlands, a girl’s body is still treated as a rite to be cut.

Kenya outlawed female genital mutilation in 2011. Yet in Kisii County the practice persists behind closed doors — increasingly “medicalized,” performed in secret by clinicians, and often disguised as something as ordinary as ear-piercing. For too many girls it ends education, forces early marriage, and opens the door to a lifetime of violence.

CECOME was born in 2012 from a simple conviction: change that lasts cannot be imposed from outside. It has to be mobilized from within — by the very families, elders and faith leaders a community already trusts.

Read our story
77% of women in Kisii have undergone FGM — among the highest rates in Kenya (KDHS 2022).
7 in 10 women across Kisii, Nyamira and Migori have been cut, despite the national ban.
Rising cases are now performed in secret by medical practitioners — harder to see, harder to stop.
Our answer: meet culture with culture. Replace the cut with an Alternative Rite of Passage that keeps the celebration — and the dignity — without the harm.

Our flagship programme

An Alternative Rite of Passage — the celebration, without the cut

Among the Abagusii, the cut has long marked a girl’s passage into womanhood. CECOME does not erase that milestone — we reimagine it. During the April and August school holidays, girls gather for a week of teaching on reproductive health, child rights, and the difference between harmful and life-giving traditions.

In December, families, chiefs and elders fill a hall to celebrate their daughters as young women — whole, healthy and back in school. The knife is replaced by knowledge, and the community itself becomes the guardian of that promise.

  • A week of mentorship in school holidays
  • Reproductive health & child-rights education
  • Public graduation that honours each girl
How the programme works
Bringing everyone to the table

Fathers, chiefs and husbands — champions, not bystanders

FGM and gender-based violence will not end while half the community stays silent. CECOME trains men as Champions of Change — fathers who refuse the cut for their daughters, elders who bless the new rite, and husbands who model households free of violence.

Alongside them, our young women’s leadership movement (through the YW4A programme) puts a new generation at the front of advocacy — on the radio, in county offices, and in the national press.
