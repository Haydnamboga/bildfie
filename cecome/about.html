Our story

Change mobilized from within.

We are the Centre for Community Mobilization and Empowerment — a woman-led, woman-managed organisation that has stood with the families of Kisii County since 2012.

How we began

Born from a community’s refusal to lose its daughters

In 2011, Kenya outlawed female genital mutilation. But in the Gusii highlands the knife did not disappear — it simply moved into the shadows. Girls were still being cut, pulled out of school and married early, while the systems meant to protect them stayed silent.

A group of local women decided that waiting was not an option. In 2012 they registered CECOME — not as outsiders arriving with answers, but as neighbours determined to mobilize the community’s own elders, men, faith leaders and young people toward a different future.

More than a decade later, that conviction still defines us. We believe the people closest to a problem are closest to its solution — and that dignity, once claimed, is not easily surrendered.

Our Mission

To support and champion the rights and dignity of vulnerable groups in the community through enabling access, advocacy, capacity building and livelihood promotion.

Our Vision

“Empowered, Resilient and Safe Communities.”

Where every woman, girl and child can live free from violence and full of possibility.

Our purpose. CECOME exists to ensure that communities attain basic development and human dignity — advancing gender equality, ending gender-based violence, and eradicating female genital mutilation across Kisii and the wider Gusii region.
What guides us

Our values

Six commitments that shape every dialogue, programme and partnership.

Dignity

Every person we serve is treated as a whole human being, never a statistic.

Community-led

Courage

We confront harmful norms and impunity, even when it is uncomfortable.

Accountability

We hold ourselves — and duty bearers — answerable for results.

Inclusion

Sustainability

We build livelihoods and local capacity so change outlives any single project.

Our journey

More than a decade of mobilizing change

From a single conviction in 2012 to a coalition reshaping policy across the county — here are the milestones along the way.

See the impact in full
2011

The law arrives — but not the change

Kenya enacts the Prohibition of FGM Act. In Kisii, the practice continues in secret.

2012

CECOME is founded

Local women register a woman-led NGO to mobilize the community from within.

2016–2020

The Alternative Rite of Passage takes root

Girls begin graduating uncut each December, blessed by their own elders.

2021

200+ girls graduate; men step up

A landmark cohort, with fathers and chiefs publicly challenged to join the fight.

2021–2023

Young women take the mic

Through YW4A, our youth carry advocacy to county offices, radio and The Standard.

2023

A coalition for change

With the EU, Solidaarisuus, Manga Heart & the Finnish Embassy, 10 grassroots CBOs are funded against GBV.

Today

Toward policy & a rescue centre

Campaigning for a county gender policy and a functional rescue centre for survivors.

Where we work

Rooted in Kisii, reaching the Gusii region

Our home is Kisii County in south-western Kenya — a green, densely populated highland region where FGM prevalence remains among the highest in the country. From our office in Kisii town, we work across wards and into neighbouring Nyamira, partnering with county government, schools, health facilities, chiefs and faith communities.
