Flagship programme

Alternative Rite of Passage

The cut has long been the Abagusii community’s marker of womanhood. Rather than dismiss that meaning, CECOME offers a powerful substitute: a week-long rite that keeps the teaching, the mentorship and the celebration — and removes the harm.

Sessions run during the April and August school holidays and culminate in a December graduation, where girls are honoured before their families, chiefs and elders as young women ready for the future.

  • Reproductive health & life skills
  • Child rights and protection from FGM & early marriage
  • Mentorship by women who have walked the path
  • A public graduation that shifts community norms

Livelihoods

Economic Empowerment

Poverty keeps families dependent on the very norms that harm their daughters. CECOME helps women form village savings and lending groups — pooling small amounts, borrowing to invest, and building businesses that change a household’s balance of power.

When a mother has her own income, she has her own voice — in her home, and in her daughter’s future. Economic independence is one of the strongest protections against FGM and gender-based violence we know.

  • Village Savings & Loan Associations (VSLAs)
  • Small-business and financial-literacy training
  • Targeted support for survivors of GBV
Education

Educational Support

FGM and early marriage are, too often, the end of a girl’s schooling. CECOME works to keep girls in the classroom — through mentorship, school-based clubs, child-rights education, and a safe route back to learning for those who have been rescued.

We also equip teachers, parents and pupils to recognise the warning signs of FGM and abuse, so that schools become the front line of protection rather than the place a girl quietly disappears from.

  • Mentorship & school clubs
  • Child-rights & protection education
  • Reintegration support after rescue

Advocacy

Community Collaboration & Advocacy

Lasting change needs more than goodwill — it needs systems that work. CECOME mobilizes moral and judicial duty bearers, confronts impunity, and pushes for the policies and institutions that protect women and girls long after a project ends.

Through the YW4A programme and partners like Equality Now, our young advocates have taken the case for a county gender policy to inter-county dialogues, local radio and TV, and the national press — and are campaigning for a functional rescue centre for survivors.

  • Campaigning for a Kisii County gender policy
  • A functional rescue centre for survivors
  • Training CSOs on social accountability & coalition building
  • Radio, TV & press to shift public norms
Woven through everything we do

Cross-cutting programmes

The work that holds the four pillars together and brings the whole community along.

Men as Champions

Engaging fathers, husbands, chiefs and faith leaders as Champions of Change who publicly reject the cut and model non-violence.

Young Women’s Leadership

Through YW4A — Awareness, Agency, Advocacy & Accountability — young women lead campaigns and hold leaders to account.

Child Protection

Rescue, referral and follow-up for girls at risk, working hand-in-hand with chiefs, police and children’s officers.

Civic Education

Helping citizens understand their rights and the law, so communities can demand the protection they are owed.

Health & Wellbeing

Linking survivors to medical and psychosocial care, and tackling the dangerous rise of medicalized FGM.

Monitoring & Evaluation

Tracking results — including digital tracking of girls through ARP — so we learn, improve and prove what works.

Our method

How community mobilization works

Change that lasts follows a path — from listening, to leading, to lasting ownership.

01

Listen & map

We start with the community’s own elders, women and youth to understand local realities.

02

Mobilize

Dialogues, champions and rites that move norms from the inside out.

03

Empower

Skills, savings and schooling that give families a stake in the change.

04

Sustain

Policy, institutions and local ownership so progress outlives us.
